# Checking the hadith text

Content step 4 (see the README). The matn in `content/hadith/` is v1's text (step 2). In this step every hadith is read against the printed book, and v1's text is corrected to what the book prints. The decisions are kept in `content/matn/NN.json`, one file per book (كتاب), so the step can be applied again on top of `tools/convert_v1.py` (`tools/matn.py apply --all`).

## How it works

1. **Comparison** (`tools/matn.py compare`). Each matn is compared word by word with three sources:
   - the OCR text of the book's pages, the only one that reads this print;
   - sunnah.com's Riyad al-Salihin, from another edition;
   - the vocalized collections (hadith-api's six books and the Muwatta), wherever the wording matches.

   The comparison lists the words in doubt, and the kinds are below. A word whose letters the OCR text confirms, and whose marks no source contradicts, is not listed. The words v1 leaves without any mark are listed too, to be given the book's marks (rule 2).
2. **Review rounds.** Every hadith is then read against a crop of its matn on the scan, starting with its doubts. The doubts alone aren't enough: in the pilot on the audit's 30 hadith they missed 4 differences, case endings no source contradicts («عالِيَةً», «فَيَحْمَدُهُ») and a wording variant. The first rounds ran at low effort and zoomed too little, so the 123 hadith they checked were checked again at medium (the owner's decision, 2026-10-23); comparing the two readings of h. 1–94 on the scan found two slips of the medium round, now fixed and in the rules (h. 21's «وسار», h. 30's «جئ»). Two medium rounds read most of their zooms too wide to show the marks: h. 74–81, which the low rounds read too and agree on every reading, and h. 169–176, which no other round read, so they were dropped to be checked again (the owner's decision, 2026-10-25; their first decisions are in `content/matn/01.json` at commit c06857c). The log now counts such zooms. A review of the next 40 medium rounds (402 hadith, 2026-11-04) found them disagreeing on a ﷺ the print leaves out and on narrators' names the print gives otherwise, which rule 1 now settles (the owner's decisions), and giving the waw of «وعَنْ» a fatha the print doesn't have (rule 3). The rounds finished on 2026-11-13, with all 1,898 hadith checked. A review of everything they left for the owner (their notes, the misprints they recorded and their reports in the log) followed the same day, and its answers are in the rules below as the owner's decisions of 2026-11-13. It compared every change of wording with the OCR text, with sunnah.com and with the collections' own text, every narrator's name with sunnah.com, and the doubtful readings with the scan; it read the older rounds' reports for h. 1–545 as well, and re-read 28 of the 47 misprints that are marks only on the scan (the rounds had read every one of them right). It found:
   - two rounds had read «هكذا وهكذا وهكذا» as two on the scan, and taken one out of the text (h. 465) or recorded a misprint the book doesn't have (h. 691): a repeated word is counted on a zoom;
   - four decisions against rules already set (h. 944, 1031, 1553 and 1670) and four the book or its sources overturn (h. 26, 785, 921 and 1525), all in rule 1;
   - «الترمذي» read two ways (rule 2);
   - twelve misprints, in ten hadith, that the rounds had to take as the book's wording (rule 1, "the print alone");
   - stretched letters, lone quotation marks and stray marks left in 57 hadith, now settled as rules 2 and 4 say (each lone quotation mark read on the scan).
3. **The second reading** ([matn-review.md](matn-review.md); the owner's decision, 2026-11-15). The rounds ran on Opus at medium effort, so every hadith is read once more against the scan as its text now stands, and every decision of the rounds is judged. Its first hadith showed why: v1 lacks a short word the print has in h. 6 («فِي فِي امْرَأَتِكَ») and h. 476 («غَطَّيْنَا بِهَا»), and no round saw it. It also gives the pilot's hadith and h. 126–168 the marks and shaddas they lack (below). It finished on 2026-11-17, with all 1,898 hadith read again and 174 of them changed.
4. **Decisions.** Each round writes its decisions to `content/matn/inbox.json`. `tools/matn.py apply` checks them, applies them to `content/hadith/`, and keeps them in `content/matn/NN.json`. A hadith with a decision there counts as checked, and `apply` drops a new decision for it. To change one, edit it in `content/matn/NN.json`, then run `tools/convert_v1.py --force` and `tools/matn.py apply --all`. `apply --all` tidies the spaces after the fixes of a hadith; a fix that doesn't match is tried again on the tidied text, since `tools/review.py` writes its fixes on tidied text.

### Kinds of doubt

| Kind | What the sources show |
|---|---|
| `missing` | The OCR text has nothing for these words of v1's. Usually the OCR lost a line; sometimes v1 adds words the book doesn't have. |
| `extra` | The OCR text has words here that v1 lacks. Usually the OCR read a line twice; sometimes the book has words v1 lacks. |
| `after` | The OCR text has words printed like the matn, with marks, after v1's last word and before the commentary. The book may print more than v1 has, such as Nawawi's explanation of a word (h. 145) or another narration (h. 830). Sometimes the OCR text has put a line of the matn out of order. |
| `split` | The same letters, spaced differently: «يارسول» / «يا رسول». |
| `stray` | A mark on no letter, such as the kasra in «اللهِ ِ ﷺ»: remove it (rule 4). |
| `dots` | The same letter shapes with other dots. Listed only where sunnah.com doesn't have v1's word. |
| `prefix`, `other` | The OCR text has another word. Most are OCR misreadings, but this is where the book's other readings are found: «للإسلام» for «إلى الإسلام», «مجاهد» for «يجاهد». |
| `tashkeel` | Every collection that has the word marks a letter differently from v1. |
| `ocr-tashkeel` | The OCR text marks a letter differently from v1, and no collection marks it as v1 does. The OCR text's marks are often wrong, but they catch v1's wrong case endings («يَسْأَلَهُ» for «يَسْأَلُهُ»). |
| `unattested` | No collection marks the word as v1 does, its last letter aside. Most of these are v1's typos: a mark on the wrong letter («قَالَوا», «سَِمعَ»). |
| `shadda` | v1 marks the word in part, but leaves bare a letter the OCR text or a collection doubles («أَن» for «أَنَّ», «متفقٌ» for «مُتَّفَقٌ»): give it the book's shadda (rule 2). |
| `align` | The OCR text has under 60% of the matn's words in order: read all of it with care. |

Not listed, though the full read still covers them: a `dots` difference where sunnah.com has v1's word; the OCR text's habitual misreadings, checked on the scan («عنهما» read «عنها», «النبي» read «الني» or «التي»); a word Tesseract reads as v1 has it; and a tashkeel conflict where the OCR text has v1's marks on the letters in question.

`tools/matn.py stats` gives the totals. Before the rounds there were 5,775 doubts, in 1,559 of the 1,868 hadith left, and 12,637 words with no mark, in 1,371 of them.

- **Speaker roles** (done, 2026-11-17). Each is settled for al-Fahl, unless `KEEP` in the tool lists it, with its reason (63): the Prophet's short replies al-Fahl doesn't quote (h. 78's «لا», «الله»), his words in another narration after «وفي رواية» (h. 101's «حُفَّت»), a verse he recites in his own words (h. 198), God's or an angel's words inside his account (h. 380, 1373), his words Nawawi cites, a Companion's words al-Fahl quotes after «وفي رواية» (h. 31, 41), and glosses and later scholars' words al-Fahl quotes.

In all, 143 hadith have role decisions, and `roles.py check` leaves no disagreement with al-Fahl that isn't kept on purpose.

The pass was for the Prophet's words, which the app sets apart; drawing that line again would touch most hadith, so the app should treat `narration` and `speech` alike (`database/README.md`).

## The rules

The book is the reference. v1's text stays wherever the book agrees with it.

1. **Letters.** Where the book's words differ from v1's, put the book's.
   - `"v1"`: v1's mistake, such as a wrong letter, words run together or split («يارسول», «أَ نَّ»), or a word added or dropped.
   - `"variant"`: v1 follows another edition, so v1's reading is also known. sunnah.com often has it too.
   - A misprint in the book (a word its own commentary shows is dropped or wrong, an impossible form) isn't copied. Keep v1's correct text and record the misprint (below). So before taking another word for a `variant`, look for v1's word in the word meanings after the matn (لغة الحديث, on a zoom of the page below it): in h. 21 the print has «وسار», but its word meanings explain «وثار: وثب ونهض». Before taking a word for a misprint, read it on a zoom and compare it with a nearby word whose spelling is certain: the OCR text drops letters too. In h. 21 it reads «يَحْزُنِي», but the zoom shows a dot over each nun of «يَحْزُنُنِي»: two, as in «يُؤَنِّبُونَنِي» (p. 44), not one, as in «يُبَشِّرُنِي» (p. 47).
   - The commentary's heading can be the slip, so weigh it against the other sources: h. 785's matn has «طرفيها», as Muslim (the source Nawawi names) and sunnah.com do, and the gloss headed «طرفها» has dropped the ya. So does h. 921's: the matn has «أَجَرَهُ», Muslim's wording, after «أْجُرْنِي», and the gloss is headed «آجره», the verb's other form. And a form that looks wrong may be the hadith's own: h. 1670's «يُحَدِّثُونَا», with one nun, is al-Bukhari's wording, and the book's word meanings explain it.
   - **The print alone against every other source** (the owner's decision, 2026-11-13). Where the print drops or changes words that v1, sunnah.com and the collection Nawawi names all have, it is a misprint, and v1's text stays, in two cases:
     - the eye has skipped from a word to its like: h. 207's «يومكم هذا، [في بلدكم هذا،] في شهركم هذا», h. 1182's «الصلاة من [الليل من] وجع», h. 928's «رسول الله ﷺ [: أن رسول الله ﷺ] قال», h. 1853 (a verse, and «وملبسه حرام»), h. 1868's «إلى إبراهيم، [فيأتون إبراهيم]»;
     - the sense breaks, or the book itself shows the word: h. 357's «فقال رسول الله ﷺ» for «فقالت: قال رسول الله ﷺ», which makes the Prophet answer those who questioned A'isha; h. 1828's «فتصرّفا» for «وتصدّقا»; h. 1137's «فأوتر» for «فأوترت», where the next narration has «قومي فأوتري»; h. 408's «عمله» for «علمه», where the book's benefits speak of knowledge; h. 492's «إن كنا ننظر» for «لننظر», which reads as a denial without the lam.

     Otherwise the print's wording is the book's (`variant`), unless Riyad al-Salihin itself has the others' (rule 8): h. 65's «إليّ», h. 1773's «في», the phrase h. 299 prints once and h. 1785's «حتى ينزع» are all in al-Fahl's edition, so the text has them. Before deciding either way, look the words up in the collection's own text and elsewhere in the book. h. 1338's «وقالا: حديث حسن صحيح» looked like a slip, and the print has «وقالا» in h. 1405 and h. 1707 too.
   - **Nawawi's words the print doesn't have** after the source line, which v1 and sunnah.com have (a gloss in h. 898, 1215 and 1687, a closing sentence in h. 1203), are another edition's and go (the owner's decision, 2026-11-13).
   - **A ﷺ the print leaves out** after the Prophet's name stays as v1 has it, and the omission is recorded as a misprint (h. 540's «قالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ:»; the owner's decision, 2026-11-04). So does a ﷺ that refers to him without his name, unless Riyad al-Salihin has none there either (rule 8): h. 962's «وقوله: «نقيها»» has none in al-Fahl's edition, so v1's ﷺ went. An honorific after anyone else follows the print: v1's ﷺ after Jibril (h. 1394, 1451) or Ibrahim (h. 1869) goes where the print has none, and the print's is added (h. 846's «آدم ﷺ»; the owner's decision, 2026-11-13).
   - **A narrator's name** the print gives otherwise than v1 is the book's (`"variant"`), with a note if you think the print is wrong. It is a misprint, and v1's name stays, only where sunnah.com has v1's name (`show` and `next` say «sunnah.com as v1») or the book itself shows the print is wrong: h. 384 has «أبي كريمة المقداد بن معديكرب», but the book gives al-Miqdad as Abu Ma'bad Ibn al-Aswad (h. 393). Where the print and sunnah.com agree, two editions of the book have the name, so it is the book's text even where the collection Nawawi names has v1's (the owner's decision, 2026-11-04), unless al-Fahl's edition has v1's (rule 8): h. 498's «خالد بن عمر» became «خالد بن عمير», as al-Fahl and Muslim have it, while h. 482's «سليمان بن سالم», h. 493's «أبي سعيد المقبري», h. 499's «أبي موسى» and h. 1080's «شقيق بن عبد الله» are al-Fahl's too and stay. Three cautions from the review of 2026-11-13:
     - `show` and `next` say «sunnah.com as v1» only for a word the print changes, not for one it adds or drops, so compare the whole name with sunnah.com's: h. 1553's «أبي زيد بن ثابت بن الضحاك» and h. 944's «طلحة بن البراء بن عازب» add a «بن» to a name sunnah.com has as v1 does, and are misprints.
     - The book itself can show the print wrong from another hadith: h. 1031's «عَبْسَةَ», against Nawawi's «بفتح العين والباء» in h. 439; h. 1525's «أبي بكر», where h. 215 gives the same sermon from Abu Bakra; h. 26's «أبي سعيد سعد بن سنان», where h. 20 names him «سعد بن مالك بن سنان».
     - sunnah.com's Arabic shares some of this print's names against its own English («أبي بكر» / Abu Bakrah in h. 1525, «جبير» / Jabr in h. 1304), so its agreement is weaker than two unrelated editions'. h. 1304's «عبد الرحمن بن جبير» stays the book's, since nothing in the book shows it wrong.
2. **Tashkeel.** Where the book and v1 both mark a letter and the marks differ, put the book's (`"v1"` if v1's mark is wrong, `"variant"` if both are good Arabic). A mark that makes an impossible form is a misprint (rule 1), not a `variant`: in h. 30 the print has «فَإِنَ آمَنْتَ», a fatha on the nun of the conditional «إنْ».
   - **A word v1 leaves bare,** with no mark at all, is written as the book prints it, with all its marks (`"marks"`; the owner's decision, 2026-10-22). `next` lists these words. Where the book leaves the word bare too, nothing changes. A word v1 marks in part keeps its marks: the book's marks on the letters v1 leaves bare aren't added.
   - **Except a shadda.** A letter the book doubles and v1 leaves bare, in a word v1 marks in part, gets the book's shadda with its vowel, because without it the word reads otherwise («أَن» / «أَنَّ», «متفقٌ» / «متَّفقٌ»; `"v1"`; the owner's decision, 2026-10-23). In h. 151, v1's «الأُسيدِيِّ» reads al-Usaydi, but the print has «الأُسَيِّدِيِّ», as Nawawi's note there spells it out, so it becomes «الأُسيِّدِيِّ». `next` lists these as `shadda` doubts. A sun letter after the article («النَّاس») and the lam of «اللَّه» read the same either way, so they stay as v1 has them.
   - The print often puts a single kasra where the grammar needs tanween (e.g. «بْنِ قَيْسِ», «بِإِسْنادِ حَسَنٍ»). That's a printing habit, so keep v1's tanween, and write the tanween in a bare word you give marks to. Where v1 has the single kasra too, write the tanween (`"v1"`, h. 23's «مِنْ ذَهَبٍ»).
   - The print also sets a sukun on a word's last letter before a connecting alif, though not every time: «مِنْ ٱلْحَجَّاجِ» (h. 92), «قَدْ ٱحْتُضِرَ» (h. 29), «عَنْ ٱبْنِ» (h. 71), but «عَنِ ٱبْنِ» (h. 75). That's a printing habit too, so write the vowel the words run on with: a kasra («قَدِ احْتُضِرَ», «عَنِ ابْنِ»), a fatha on «مِنَ» before the article («مِنَ الْحَجَّاجِ»), a damma on a plural «ـمُ» («هُمُ الَّذِينَ»). Where v1 has the sukun too, write the vowel (`"v1"`; the owner's decision, 2026-10-25). The sukun stays where the words don't run on: before a colon or a quotation («قَالَتْ: اتَّقِ»), on a ha of pause («يَا أُمَّهْ اصْبِرِي», h. 30), and in a foreign name («بَرْدِزْبَهْ», h. 1).
   - When you write a word as the book prints it, leave out two more habits of the print. It sets a wasla on a connecting alif, but unevenly (p. 65 has «مِنَ ٱلسُّفُنِ» and «ٱلصَّغِيرِ», but «فِي الْأَرْضِ»): write a plain alif, as the whole text does. And it stretches a word to fill out a line («عَـــنِ», h. 1349): write it without the stretch. Take the stretch out of v1's words too («لصـاحِبِهِ», h. 313; `"v1"`).
   - In this print a kasra with a shadda is set under the shadda, above the letter, so on a zoom it can look like a damma («المُزَنِيِّ», h. 14). A damma sits on top of the shadda («قَطُّ», h. 20). Before taking a case ending for a misprint, compare its mark with a word on the same page whose case is certain. The ta of «الترمذي» is such a mark: in the scan's own pixels the shadda and its vowel are one blot (p. 1142), and the rounds read it both ways. So the word keeps v1's «التِّرْمِذِيُّ» everywhere (rule 7), and the 24 fixes that had made it «التَّرْمِذِيُّ» were taken back (the owner's decision, 2026-11-13).
   - Where the book leaves a letter bare, keep v1's mark, unless it is plainly wrong (a mark on the wrong letter, «يْومٍ» for «يَوْمٍ»). Then write the word correctly, as the collections have it (`"v1"`).
3. **A word you change or give marks to** is written as the book prints it, with all its marks, read on a zoom, and with no mark the print doesn't have. The print leaves bare the waw that opens a word («وعَنْ», «وفِي», «وقالَ»): don't give it a fatha. Earlier rounds did in about 130 fixes, which are left as they are, since the fatha doesn't change the reading.
4. **Not checked:** spelling that doesn't change the reading, where v1's spelling stays (مائة / مئة, داود / داوود, السماوات / السموات, الزنا / الزنى, يا بن / يا ابن, final ى / ي, هكذا / هٰكذا, أن لا / ألّا). A letter that changes the reading isn't spelling: v1's «جئ» for the book's «جِيءَ» (h. 30) loses the long vowel, so it is a `"v1"` fix. Also not checked: punctuation; and the speaker roles, which a pass of their own checks after the rounds (above). The marks on such a word are still checked (rule 2): where the book prints «سَبْعِ مِئَةِ» and v1 «سَبْعِمَائِةِ», write «سَبْعِمِائَةِ». But remove a stray mark that breaks the text, such as a verse reference in brackets («[آل عمران: 77]»), which the book gives in a footnote. A lone « or »: where the print has the pair, add the half v1 lacks (h. 463); otherwise remove it (h. 456).
5. **The whole matn** is checked: the narrator, the hadith, Nawawi's source line («متفق عليه», «رواه الترمذي وقال: حديث حسن»), and his explanation of words after it («المِرْطُ: كساء …»). The book sometimes prints more of it than v1 has, such as Nawawi's whole explanation (h. 145) or another narration (`after` doubts): add what v1 lacks, as printed (`"v1"`).
   - **A source line the print leaves out** stays as v1 has it. Where the book's commentary shows it, the print dropped it: record a misprint (h. 31, whose commentary gives the hadith from both al-Bukhari and Muslim for v1's «متفق عليه»). Otherwise say so in a note (h. 143, whose commentary cites only al-Bukhari).
   - **Not the ordinals.** In some chapters the book numbers the hadith in words («فالأول:», «الثاني:», «الثاني عشر:»), after «وأما الأحاديث» before the first. They aren't part of the matn: the app shows every hadith's number, and «فالأول» goes on from the chapter's introduction, which the app doesn't show. `tools/convert_v1.py` removes the 29 v1 has, and they aren't added where v1 lacks them (the owner's decision, 2026-10-23).
6. **Hadith not in v1** (153, 172, 1126 and 1548) were transcribed from the scan in step 3. Check every mark of theirs at zoom.
7. **If a word can't be read** even zoomed, keep v1's and say so in a note, unless Riyad al-Salihin settles it (rule 8).
8. **Where the print is in dispute, Riyad al-Salihin itself decides** (the owner's decision, 2026-11-17). The print is *Nuzhat al-Muttaqin*, a commentary on *Riyad al-Salihin*, so its hadith text should be Riyad's. Where the print and v1 have different readings (`variant`), the print looks misprinted, the scan can't settle a reading, or the print has words the collections and sunnah.com don't (or lacks theirs), the text follows Maher al-Fahl's critical edition of Riyad al-Salihin (Dar Ibn Kathir, 2007; `tools/riyad.py`, which finds the words in its text). The fix is `"riyad"`, and where the text now leaves the print, the print's words go to `corrections` as a misprint, with al-Fahl's page as the reason. Where al-Fahl leaves the letters in question bare, or has the text's reading, the earlier decision stands.
   - **Applied to everything the review left** (2026-11-17): every change and misprint of the second reading, and every note of the rounds. `tools/riyad.py judge` weighed the 240 changes and misprints: al-Fahl has the text's reading in 114, the other in 33 (29 of them the print's `variant`s, now v1's reading again, which al-Fahl shares), and leaves 84 undecided (mostly bare words, which are no dispute, or a misprint grammar settles). ### The decision for a hadith

`content/matn/inbox.json` holds one entry per hadith, keyed by its number:

```json
{
 "84": {"fix": [["يأْتِي النبيِّ", "يَأْتِي النَّبِيَّ", "v1"]]},
 "85": {},
 "145": {"fix": [["عن أبي هريرة", "عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ", "marks"]]},
 "597": {"fix": [["قال : سَمِعْتُ", "قالَ : سَمِعْتُ", "marks"]],
         "misprint": [["التَّقِيَّ الْغَنِيَّ", "التَّقِيَّ الغَنِيَّ الْخَفِيَّ", "The print drops «الخفي»; its own word meanings explain «الخفي: الخامل الذكر», and Muslim has it."]]},
 "691": {"fix": [["قالَ لي النَّبِيُّ", "قالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ", "variant"]]}
}
```

- `{}`: the matn is right as it is.
- `fix`: `[from, to, why]`, with why `"v1"`, `"variant"`, `"marks"` (the fix only gives the book's marks to words v1 leaves bare) or `"riyad"` (Riyad al-Salihin's reading where the print is in dispute, rule 8). `from` is copied from the matn as printed by `next`; the order of the marks on a letter doesn't matter. It must occur once in the matn, so take a neighbouring word with it when needed. It may run over the speaker roles («قَالَ: لا، قُلْتُ»): `apply` keeps each word in its role. `to` is empty to delete. Where a hadith has many bare words, one fix can take a whole phrase.
- `misprint`: `[printed, correction, reason]` for a misprint in the book. The matn keeps the correct text, and the misprint goes to the hadith's `corrections`.
- `note`: a short English note for the owner, added to the hadith's `notes`. Use it only for something the owner should know or decide.

## One round

`tools/long_run.sh` starts a fresh Claude Code session for each round, with the prompt "Do one round of the matn check: follow docs/matn.md exactly." (README, "The matn check's long run"). A round checks up to 12 hadith, fewer when they're long (800 words of matn in all), and stops. A hadith longer than that is a round of its own (h. 21, with 1,608 words): that's expected, so don't report it. Check every hadith `next` gives you. Rounds are kept short because every step re-reads everything the session has read so far.

1. **Read** this file and nothing else that the round doesn't need. The tools' usage is here, so don't read their source.
2. If `content/matn/inbox.json` exists, an earlier round didn't finish. Run `.venv/bin/python tools/matn.py apply`, fix what it reports in the inbox, and run it again until it applies. Then go on.
3. **Run** `.venv/bin/python tools/matn.py next`. It prints how many hadith are checked, then the round's hadith. For each one it gives:
   - the crops of its matn on the scan: one image per page, with the page and the part of the page the crop covers;
   - v1's matn;
   - its doubts;
   - the words v1 leaves with no mark, run by run.

   If it says all are checked, stop.
4. **Read all the round's crops at once**: one message with a Read call for each crop. Every step re-reads everything before it, so reading them one by one costs several times as much. Then, for each hadith, read the matn on the scan word by word against v1's text:
   - Check that its crops hold the whole matn: from the hadith's number in the margin (its number in the chapter over its number in the book, e.g. ٤ over ١٤٥) to where the commentary starts, in smaller print without marks («الحديث رواه …», «لغة الحديث», «أفاد الحديث»), or to the next hadith. When the tools can't place a matn, its crop is the whole page: find the hadith by its number. If a crop cuts the matn off, zoom out over a wider range of the page.
   - Settle each listed doubt first, then any other difference you see, and read on to the end of the matn: the book can print more than v1 has (rule 5).
   - Give the book's marks to the words v1 leaves bare (rule 2).
   - **Zoom on every word you change or give marks to,** and on any other mark you're not sure of: a crop is too small to be sure of every mark. `tools/zoom.py PAGE TOP BOTTOM --left L --right R` takes the page and fractions of it: the crop's range is printed next to it, and a line is about 0.035 of the page. A zoom takes a line or two, at most half the page wide: a wider one shows little more than the crop, and `zoom.py` says so. Do all of a page's spots in one command, with `--crop TOP BOTTOM LEFT RIGHT` for each, and read all the zooms of the round together too.
5. **Write** `content/matn/inbox.json` with a decision for each of the round's hadith, in one Write.
6. **Run** `.venv/bin/python tools/matn.py apply`. It keeps the entries that failed in the inbox and says why. Correct them and run apply again.
7. **Stop** with a one-line summary: the hadith checked, and how many fixes for each reason (`v1`, `variant`, `marks`).

**What a round may do.** Read any file, write `content/matn/inbox.json`, and run the project's tools as plain commands from `book_data/`, where the round starts, e.g. `.venv/bin/python tools/zoom.py 96 0.30 0.35`. Write no `cd`, pipes, `python -c` or other shell commands: anything else is refused, because nobody is there to approve it. Never commit to git, and never change `docs/` or `tools/`. If a rule or a tool seems wrong, say so in the summary and carry on with the rules as they are.
